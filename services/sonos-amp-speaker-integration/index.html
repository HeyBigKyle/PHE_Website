Before the amp

Understand the speakers before choosing the amp.

  1. 01

    Existing speakers & cable routes

    We identify accessible speaker models, wire endpoints and any wall volume controls or selector boxes. Unknown wiring and missing model information need assessment before equipment is connected.

  2. 02

    One zone or several

    Decide which rooms should play the same music and which need independent sources or volume. One amplifier is not automatically a separate audio zone for every connected room.

  3. 03

    Amplifier & speaker compatibility

    Speaker impedance, quantity, wiring and the amplifier's supported load matter. We follow the manufacturer's guidance for the specific configuration rather than connecting every speaker to one output.

  4. 04

    TV audio & everyday use

    If you want the same speakers for TV sound, include the TV model and existing sound equipment. We review supported connections, placement and the intended use before configuring app control and room grouping.

FAQ

Sonos Amp & existing speakers questions.

Can Sonos Amp work with existing ceiling speakers?

It can power compatible passive speakers, subject to the supported load and wiring configuration. Existing speakers are not automatically compatible; we review their requirements, condition and connections.

Can one Amp give every room different music?

Not simply because multiple rooms have speakers. Independent playback zones require suitable amplification and system design. Explain which spaces should operate separately before equipment is selected.

Can I reuse old speaker wiring and volume controls?

Possibly. The wire routes, condition, connected controls and speaker load need review. Do not assume an old selector or volume control is suitable for the new amplifier.

Should I choose Sonos, WiiM or HEOS?

Start with your existing equipment, music services, TV connections and control preferences. Our whole-home audio comparison explains the planning differences; the final choice depends on your system.
