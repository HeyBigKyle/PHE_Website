What to Plan Before Mounting a TV With a Soundbar

Plan the screen, soundbar and connections together before drilling, so the finished installation looks clean and stays practical to use.

A clean TV installation involves more than hiding the wires. The screen needs a comfortable position, the soundbar needs room to work, and the connections need to remain accessible. Planning those details together can help avoid a wall layout that looks finished but is awkward to use or service.

Before a TV and soundbar installation in your Greater Phoenix home, settle the equipment models and mark the proposed arrangement from your normal seats. Then review mounting, power, signal connections, and access as one project.

Choose the viewing position before the wall layout

Start with the seats you use most. Mark the proposed screen outline with removable tape and look at it while seated. Include the soundbar outline and any cabinet below it. This makes it easier to compare the complete arrangement before holes are drilled.

Consider daylight at the times you normally watch television. In a Phoenix living room, windows and shade positions may influence the preferred wall or viewing angle. Check the room during the day as well as in the evening rather than choosing a location from a photograph alone.

Avoid setting the screen height only by centering it in the available wall space. Ask the installer to explain the proposed height in relation to the seating, furniture, and soundbar. PHE’s premium TV and AV installation service addresses the display and supporting audio together.

Leave the clearance required by the actual soundbar

A soundbar is not simply a decorative strip that can be fitted into any gap beneath a TV. Its position needs to follow the selected model’s instructions, including clearances and the location of its controls and speakers.

For example, Sonos recommends a minimum 4-inch gap between a wall-mounted Arc Ultra and the TV, with at least 1 foot between each end of the soundbar and a wall or other obstruction. Those are Arc Ultra requirements, not universal dimensions for every soundbar. Use the manual for the exact product before finalizing the wall layout. Sonos Arc Ultra wall-mount guidance

Discuss any shelf, cabinet, or moving TV mount that could affect the arrangement. If the soundbar will move with the screen, confirm that the mounting accessories and cable movement are suitable for the specific equipment.

Confirm the TV audio connection before concealing cables

List the exact TV and soundbar models and identify the TV’s audio ports. An HDMI socket is not automatically the correct connection for returning TV sound to the soundbar. The proposed signal path should be clear before wiring is concealed.

Sonos states that its Atmos-capable Arc, Arc SL, Arc Ultra, and Beam (Gen 2) require HDMI ARC or HDMI eARC for Dolby Atmos playback. It also explains that using its optical adapter instead removes HDMI-CEC TV control. These are reasons to check compatibility and intended functions rather than treating optical and HDMI as interchangeable. Sonos TV connection requirements

Share the devices you actually watch: built-in TV apps, a streaming player, cable box, disc player, or game console. Have the installer verify the complete path and settings for those sources. Do not assume that a soundbar’s format support guarantees the same audio result from every source connected to every TV.

If Sonos is part of the project, explore PHE’s Sonos installation service alongside the TV mounting plan.

Plan access as carefully as cable concealment

Ask where power, HDMI, network, and any other required connections will be located. The plan should identify what can be reused and what needs new wiring or access work. Confirm the scope of any electrical work and wall repair separately.

A low-profile mount can create a clean finish, but it also makes access worth discussing. How will someone reach the TV ports, replace a cable, or remove the display for service? If connection equipment sits in a cabinet, make sure the location follows that equipment’s ventilation and access instructions.

For a moving mount, discuss the full range of motion and the cable route while the screen moves. Cables and connectors should be considered in that moving arrangement, not only when the TV is pushed back against the wall.

Include everyday controls in the installation scope

Decide which remote or interface you want to use for normal viewing. Ask how the TV, soundbar, and sources will turn on, how volume will be adjusted, and how another household member will change activities.

These questions become especially useful when replacing a receiver or combining existing equipment with a new display. PHE’s AV integration approach considers how the components work together, while control system planning can address supported ways to simplify a larger setup.

Test the sources you use before the walkthrough ends

Watch something familiar from each important source. Confirm picture, dialogue, volume control, and source switching from your normal seat. If a particular audio format is part of the agreed scope, ask the installer to verify the format received using the selected equipment’s status display or app.

Have someone else in the household repeat the basic steps. Check that the installation includes an understandable way to stop watching and return to the next activity. A short demonstration using your own sources is more useful than testing only one video chosen for the installation.
